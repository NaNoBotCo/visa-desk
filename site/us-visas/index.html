What we do, and what we don't

  • We assemble the file: the evidence, the civil documents, the certified translations, the timeline, the affidavits.
  • We write the parts that are written: the business plan, the source-of-funds narrative, the job-creation and economic analysis, the personal statement.
  • We prepare you for the interview, in Thai or in English.
  • Legal advice and the filings themselves go through a US immigration attorney. We work alongside one; we are not one.

Categories

Two tags, and they are ours rather than anyone's ruling: Most asked for is what people walk in with. Biggest file is where the paperwork is longest and the outcome lasts longest.

Visit Study and exchange Work Family Immigrate and invest Refusals, waivers and after the visa

Visit

Short stays: business, family, a holiday, a layover.

  • B-1 Business visitor Meetings, a conference, a trade fair, negotiating a contract. Most asked for
  • B-2 Holiday and family visit A holiday, a wedding, a graduation, time with family. Most asked for
  • B-1/B-2 Combined One file covering both reasons to travel.
  • C-1/D Transit and crew Air and sea crew, and passing through on the way elsewhere.

Study and exchange

University, vocational training, and the exchange programmes.

  • F-1 Academic student Degree and language programmes. The I-20, the funding evidence, the interview. Most asked for
  • M-1 Vocational student Trade and technical courses that are not academic degrees.
  • J-1 Exchange visitor Au pair, intern, trainee, camp counsellor, research scholar, teacher. Most asked for
  • F-2 · M-2 · J-2 Dependents Spouse and children travelling with a student or exchange visitor.

Work

An employer, a transfer, a talent, or your own company.

  • E-1 · E-2 Treaty trader and treaty investor The Thailand route we know best: the business plan, the source-of-funds narrative, the company formation and the economic case that the file stands or falls on. Biggest file
  • H-1B Specialty occupation The employer files; we assemble your side of it.
  • H-2A · H-2B Seasonal work Agricultural and non-agricultural seasonal jobs, when the programme is open to Thai nationals.
  • L-1A · L-1B Intracompany transfer Moving from a Thai company to its US office, as a manager or a specialist.
  • O-1 Extraordinary ability Evidence of a record: press, prizes, published work, the people who will speak for you.
  • P-1 · P-2 · P-3 Athletes and performers Muay Thai, dance, music, and culturally unique performance.
  • R-1 Religious worker Monks, teachers and staff invited by a US temple or congregation.
  • Q-1 Cultural exchange Sharing Thai culture through a sponsoring US programme.

Family

Marriage, engagement, parents and children.

  • K-1 Fiancé(e) The relationship evidence, the timeline, the affidavit, the interview. Most asked for
  • CR-1 · IR-1 Spouse Married already. The petition packet, the civil documents, the translations. Most asked for
  • K-3 Spouse, while the petition runs Prepared when the timing makes it worth doing.
  • IR-2 · IR-5 Children and parents Children under 21, and parents of a US citizen over 21.
  • F1 · F2A · F2B · F3 · F4 Preference categories Adult children, siblings, and the family of green card holders.

Immigrate and invest

The routes that end in a green card.

  • EB-5 Investor Source-of-funds narrative, the job-creation and economic analysis, and a business plan built to the standard the adjudicator reads it against. Biggest file
  • EB-2 NIW National interest waiver The case for why your work matters enough to skip the job offer. Biggest file
  • EB-1 Extraordinary ability, professor, executive The evidence file, built the way the criteria are actually read.
  • EB-3 Skilled, professional, other worker With an employer behind it, and the labour certification that comes first.
  • EB-4 Special immigrant Including religious workers with a US congregation behind them.
  • DV Diversity Visa lottery The entry prepared and checked, in the years Thailand is on the list. Free to enter; the mistakes are what disqualify people.

Refusals, waivers and after the visa

The second attempt, and what comes once you are there.

  • 214(b) After a refusal What changed since the last interview, written down and evidenced, before you pay the fee again. Most asked for
  • I-601 · I-601A Waivers Prepared with US counsel, who files it.
  • Renewal Renewal and interview waiver Same visa, new passport, less trouble than the first time.
  • I-131 · N-400 Re-entry permit and naturalisation For green card holders keeping a foot in both countries.
